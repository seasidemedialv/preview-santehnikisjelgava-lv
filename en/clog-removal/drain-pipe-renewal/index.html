The post-war city and the timber houses around Vecpilsētas iela

In the fighting of the Second World War in 1944, almost 90% of Jelgava was destroyed, and after the war the city was rebuilt in the Soviet style. That is why many blocks of flats are post-war buildings, and their drains are usually cast-iron soil stacks with cast-iron or early plastic branches.

Around Vecpilsētas iela part of the 18th–19th century timber architecture has survived. In such houses the drain was usually laid later than the building was put up, often through the floor or the basement, so a renewal first has to find where the pipe runs and where it leaves into the yard.

In Pārlielupe, the residential buildings of the RAF district were built in the late 1970s for workers of the Riga Bus Factory. In a multi-storey building the soil stack runs through every storey, so it is renewed in storey sections, flat by flat.

Point 20: the owner stops leaks in their own system

Jelgava City binding regulations No. 19-10 (point 20) require the owner or holder to stop, at their own expense, any leaks in the water supply and sewer system of their property. A sewer leak also slowly damages the floor and the ceiling below it.

A lengthwise crack in a cast-iron pipe cannot be mended, so that section is replaced. If only the area near a cold-water pipe is damp, it may be condensation rather than sewage, and then no renewal is needed.

Point 53: roof downpipe away from the household outlet

Regulations No. 19-10 (point 53) required users whose rainwater went into the household sewer to rebuild the outlet within five years of 2 August 2019 and build a separate connection for rainwater to the city’s drainage and storm sewer system.

If you are renewing the cast-iron outlet of an old house, this is the moment to disconnect the roof downpipe or yard gully from it. Ask Jelgavas ūdens where rainwater may be connected before the work, not after it.

Example: a soil stack section in the RAF district

In a 4th-floor flat in the RAF district, a section of cast-iron soil stack is replaced and a toilet and bath branch is connected to it. The job is one soil stack section (from €100 per storey section) and two connection points (from €30 each).

At least €160 in total, labour price, materials extra. Whether the trip is charged separately, and how to reach the soil stack in the neighbouring flats, is settled on that same call before the job.

Questions people ask

Why is the soil stack in a post-war block of flats renewed storey by storey?

In a multi-storey building the soil stack runs through every storey, so it is renewed in storey sections, flat by flat: a soil stack section (Ø110) from €100 per storey section. The soil stack is usually common property of the building, unless the building’s documents say otherwise, so the work is agreed with the building manager.

Why is a CCTV inspection report needed after a yard outlet is renewed?

Regulations No. 19-10 (point 12) require a CCTV inspection report of the pipeline for new or rebuilt gravity sewer systems before they are put into service. It shows that the pipe has no sags and the joints are intact.

How deep must a new yard drain pipe lie in Jelgava?

In the Jelgava row of LBN 003-19, the frost depth for clayey soil is 59 cm measured with a 2-year return period and 119 cm over a 100-year period; in sandy soil it is greater. The pipe has to lie where it does not freeze; the exact depth is set by the design or the technical conditions.
