Meter or norm: who pays for a running toilet

Jelgava regulations No. 19-10 (point 20) say that the owner is responsible for leaks in the system on their property and fixes them at their own cost. A leaking cistern is also lost water, and the owner pays for it.

Where there is no meter, consumption is calculated by the norms in the regulations, not by how much water actually runs away: 150 l a day per resident in a block of flats, 215 l in a house with a bath, while for a house in the municipality JNKU charges 15 m³ a month per declared person. Where there is a meter, its reading shows a running cistern.

Jelgava City binding regulations No. 19-10, points 18, 20 and 34.2 and Annex 1; Jelgavas novada KU frequently asked questions. Checked 19 November 2026.

Block of flats without a meter: 150 l a day

The same annex, for a house with central water, sewerage and hot water; 90 l for a building without baths and showers.

Supply to the cistern: at least 8 mm
the minimum diameter for a flushing cistern in LBN 221-15; a thin, kinked hose can fill the cistern more slowly.

After a water shut-off: sediment in the fill valve

For an interruption of supply caused by planned repairs, the service provider informs customers no later than three days in advance (regulations No. 19-10, point 18). After the shut-off and restart, sediment moves in the pipes and settles in the strainer of the fill valve.

The cistern then fills much more slowly or does not shut off fully. The strainer can be taken out and rinsed; if the valve membrane has hardened, the whole fill valve is replaced, and that is flush mechanism work from €35.

A high-level cistern on the wall

If the toilet still has a high-level cistern on the wall with a pipe down to the pan, there are two routes. If the cistern stays, only the inner mechanism is changed, and universal fill valves with a side or bottom inlet also fit.

If the high-level cistern is swapped for a compact cistern on the pan, the place of the supply changes, and the cistern swap includes a new point from €35. If the toilet has to be changed too, fitting is from €50.

Swapping the flush mechanism: valve and pressure

Jelgava regulations No. 19-10 (point 14) set at least 2.6 bar at the inlet at ground level. If a new cistern still fills slowly, the fault is usually a blocked fill valve strainer or a half-closed angle valve, not the city’s pressure.

Before the toilet’s flush mechanism is replaced, a photo of the cistern with the lid off helps: it shows whether the mechanism has a button or a lever and what size the flush valve is.

Example: a three-storey building in Mežciems

In Mežciems, next to the former forestry complex, two three-storey residential buildings of 24 flats each were built. If in a flat like that the cistern runs constantly and the base of the toilet is damp, the job is made up of two items.

Swapping the flush mechanism from €35 and stopping the leak at the cuff from €40: at least €75 for the work, parts extra. The plumber tells you about the trip to Mežciems on the call before the job.

Questions people ask

Does a running toilet cost more in a flat without a meter?

The bill is then usually worked out by the norm in regulations No. 19-10, 150 l a day per resident in a block of flats, while the difference between the building’s meter and the flats is usually shared by all the owners; ask the building manager how it works in your building. The owner is responsible for fixing a leak in their own system (point 20), unless the building’s documents say otherwise.

Can wet wipes be flushed down the toilet in Jelgava?

Regulations No. 19-10 (point 34.2) forbid discharging into the sewer substances that block the networks, including textiles. Wipes do not break down like toilet paper, so they go in the bin.

Can the high-level cistern stay and only the mechanism be replaced?

It can. Universal fill valves fit with a side or bottom inlet, and the flush mechanism is chosen to suit the cistern opening. The cistern stays in place, and the supply is not moved.
