Fitting or swapping a floor-standing toilet costs from €50, a wall-hung toilet on a concealed frame from €85, labour price, materials extra.

A toilet in the basement: a fixture below the nearest chamber cover

LBN 221-15 says a fixture sitting lower than the lid of the nearest inspection chamber (a basement toilet, for example) must be connected to the sewer separately: with its own outlet and a device that stops water from the street network flowing back into the basement. A basement toilet is often lower than the nearest chamber, and then this requirement applies to it.

The city regulations No. 19-10 (point 23) add to this: if the building is in Jelgava’s flood-prone area and is connected to the central sewer, a backflow valve must be built into its outlet. So before a toilet goes into a basement, find out whether the house is in such an area, and allow for it in the price of the outlet. For basement outlets and pumps, read the page Basement pumps.

A toilet in a garden house: the holding tank must be registered within 1 month

If a new toilet in an allotment, a summer house or a sauna sends wastewater not into the city network but into a holding tank or septic tank, the work does not end at the toilet. SIA Jelgavas ūdens states that sealed holding tanks, septic tanks with two or more chambers and small treatment plants must be registered, also at summer houses, cottages and saunas; failure to register is an administrative offence.

Jelgava City binding regulations No. 19-17 (point 3.1.1) require the first registration application to be submitted within 1 month of the system coming into use, and this can be done at dks.jelgava.lv. In April 2023 there were 1,571 such systems registered in Jelgava, although according to compiled information there are about 4,000.

In the villages of Jelgava Municipality, for example Ozolnieki or Kalnciems, the municipality’s binding regulations No. 5 (2021) on decentralised sewage apply; the register there is kept by SIA Jelgavas novada KU, which registers systems free of charge.

Pressure, screens and repair leftovers

Pressure at the inlet: 2.6 bar
Jelgava regulations No. 19-10 (point 14) set at least 0.26 MPa at the inlet at ground level at maximum consumption; the fill valve’s requirements are compared with this figure, if the manufacturer states them.
Screens: 3 mm
at the Jelgava treatment plant, automatic screens with 3 mm gaps remove rags, sand and grease (SIA Jelgavas ūdens); everything that goes into the toilet travels along the network to that point.
Forbidden substances: points 34.2. and 34.3
regulations No. 19-10 forbid discharging plaster, sand, soil, textiles, oils and unground food waste into the sewer; after a repair this also covers plaster and sand leftovers that would be handy to pour down the toilet.
Basement outlet: at least 20 ‰
LBN 221-15 requires sewer outlets from basement rooms to have a fall of at least 2 %, or 2 cm per metre.

Post-war standard buildings and the 100 mm outlet

In the fighting of the Second World War in 1944, almost 90 % of Jelgava was destroyed, and in the 1950s the empty plots along the streets were filled with standard residential buildings. Standard large-panel buildings were built in the city until the 1980s. In such buildings there may still be a cast-iron outlet under the old toilet, but for the new toilet LBN 221-15 provides for a connection of at least 100 mm.

Urinal: a new supply only after the meter

A urinal needs its own water supply and its own trap. If the supply is run from scratch, Jelgava regulations No. 19-10 (point 15.8.) forbid connecting it before the commercial metering unit, so the new point goes after the meter.

The outlet diameter, the height from the floor and the type of flushing are set by the installation sheet of the particular urinal’s manufacturer, so it is worth attaching it to the request. Each new water or drain connection is from €35 per outlet.

Example: a wall-hung toilet in a house in Romas krogs

Romas krogs consists entirely of houses, and in Dainas iela the water supply and sewer were built in 2018. If in a house like this a floor-standing toilet is swapped for a wall-hung one on a frame and the outlet stays in the old place, the labour is the frame with the toilet, from €85. The frame needs an open wall or a false wall, so this is decided before tiling; swapping a floor-standing toilet in the old place would be from €50.

If the outlet has to be raised or moved by one position, from €35 per outlet is added. In total at least €120 for the work, the frame, toilet and panel extra. On the call you go through the job and the price before the work.

Questions people ask

Does a garden house toilet connected to a holding tank need registering if the house is used only in summer?

Yes. SIA Jelgavas ūdens states that sealed holding tanks must be registered also at summer houses and cottages, and regulations No. 19-17 require it to be done within 1 month of the start of use.

Does a dry toilet in an allotment need registering too?

Yes. SIA Jelgavas ūdens states that any private sewage system must be registered, including a dry toilet in an allotment or on an unbuilt plot; this can be done at dks.jelgava.lv.

Is a pressure of 2.6 bar enough for a new toilet?

Regulations No. 19-10 set at least 2.6 bar at the inlet at ground level, not on an upper floor. The working pressure of the fill valve is stated by its manufacturer; if the cistern fills slowly, first check the valve strainer and the angle valve.
