Plumber in Jaunsvirlauka - a parish where every village has its own networks

Six villages and three different sets of networks: that is how Jaunsvirlauka Parish, on the left bank of the Lielupe, bordering the city of Jelgava and centred on Staļģene, looks in the municipality’s environmental report.

Plumbing work in Jaunsvirlauka therefore depends first on which village the house is in.

Village by village: water main and sewerage

On the municipality’s page, the villages of Jaunsvirlauka Parish are Staļģene, Dzirnieki, Mežciems, Kārniņi, Jaunsvirlauka and Vecsvirlauka. The environmental report of the municipality’s spatial plan (LVĢMC data from 2014 and other data from 2016) shows the following picture for them:

In Jaunsvirlauka and Vecsvirlauka: water yes, wastewater to a tank

The village of Jaunsvirlauka lies in the south of the parish by the V1038 road, 8 km from Staļģene, and grew up around the Jaunsvirlauka manor (Neu-Bergfried). If a village has a water main but no central sewerage, a house’s wastewater goes to its own septic tank, holding tank or small treatment unit.

These systems have their own municipal rules (No. 5) and are registered with Jelgavas novada KU. The plumber’s part is the house outlet and the yard line up to the tank: its fall, inspection points and cleaning.

If the line to the tank clogs, it is jetted with a high-pressure stream fed in through an inspection point; that is from €60 per hour (1 h minimum). Clearing the yard drain line is from €50 per call-out (1st hour). That is the labour price, materials extra; if the trip to the parish villages is charged separately, the plumber mentions it on the call before the job. More on the page Sewer jetting.

For a long yard line to the tank, it is worth providing inspection points at the house wall and before bends. Through them a jetting hose or a camera can be fed straight toward the blockage, and the line does not have to be dug up to find where it is blocked.

Dzirnieki: sewerage but no central water main

In Dzirnieki the situation is the opposite: the environmental report lists central sewerage there without a central water main. A house’s water then comes from another source, for example its own well or borehole, while the wastewater goes into the village network. So the house outlet and its connection to the street network are the same here as in the city, but the water side is entirely the house’s own matter.

In such a house the plumber works downstream of the water source: pipes to the taps, the water heater, sinks and drains up to the house sewer outlet. If the water comes from a well or borehole, the pipe from the source to the house has to be laid deep enough; we explain the frost depth rows for Jelgava on the page Plumber in Dalbe.

Staļģene: the parish centre and the division

In 2021 Staļģene had 474 residents, the village is 7 m above sea level, and by our map reading it lies about 17 km from Jelgava’s centre. The parish centre is in Staļģene, and the Staļģene division of Jelgavas novada KU is also named after it.

The Staļģene division of Jelgavas novada KU serves Jaunsvirlauka, Vircava and Platone parishes. The parish covers 123.0 km², and in 2026 it had 2,404 residents. The parish’s rivers are the Īslīce, Lielupe, Liepars, Rēda, Sesava, Svitene and Vircava; the Īslīce (L153) is on the list of flood-risk rivers of the Lielupe basin. Mežciems and Kārniņi lie close to the city’s southern boundary, and they have their own pages.

Questions people ask

Dzirnieki has sewerage but no central water main. What is the plumber's job, then?

The water side downstream of the source itself: pipes to the taps, the water heater and sinks. The sewer side is the same as in a village with both networks: the house outlet up to the village network, its fall and cleaning.

Which Jelgavas novada KU division serves Jaunsvirlauka Parish?

The Staļģene division. It also serves Vircava and Platone parishes.

The yard line to a holding tank in Vecsvirlauka keeps clogging. What is done about it?

The line is jetted with a high-pressure stream fed in through an inspection point. If the blockage keeps coming back, the pipe is looked at with a camera to see whether it has a sagging section or a crack.
